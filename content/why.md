## The library is enormous. The shelves are mostly empty.

There are an estimated **30 to 40 million** public domain works in existence —
books whose copyrights have expired, belonging to everyone and no one.
Digitizing them has been the work of dedicated volunteers for over half a
century.

The results tell a story of how far we still have to go.

**Project Gutenberg**, founded in 1971, has produced roughly 78,000
electronic texts in 54 years. That is a monumental achievement — and it
represents about **0.2%** of what exists.

**Standard Ebooks**, founded around 2015, takes Gutenberg texts and transforms
them into beautifully designed, professionally typeset ebooks. In 11 years,
they have produced **1,398 titles** — roughly 0.004% of the public domain.

The gap between what *could* be read and what *can* be read, in a format that
does justice to the work, is immense.

## A matter of pace

Standard Ebooks produces a book through careful, labor-intensive volunteer
work. A typical title takes **three to six months** from source selection to
publication. At that rate — about 127 books per year — matching Gutenberg's
78,000 titles would take over a century.

We believed there had to be a faster way without sacrificing quality.

## Our approach

We built a production pipeline that brings the time per book from months down
to **days** — sometimes a single day. It does not replace human judgment; it
eliminates the repetitive mechanical work that slows down manual production.

Every book is sourced from a reliable transcription, compared against original
page scans to correct errors, and enhanced with proper typography, semantic
structure, and accessibility features. The output is an EPUB that stands
alongside the best available editions — and the build process is fully
reproducible from source.

## What this means

At a production rate of 3–5 books per day, the math changes:

- In **under a year**, we can match Standard Ebooks' entire catalog
- In **five years**, we could produce over 9,000 carefully edited titles
- The bottleneck shifts from production capacity to curation and sourcing

This is not a race against other projects. Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks have
done extraordinary work, and we stand on their shoulders. But the size of the
problem demands more than volunteer hours can deliver in a human lifetime.

## What you can do

Every book we publish is open source. The source code for each book lives on
[GitHub](https://github.com/Impression-Editions). You can inspect how it was
made, suggest corrections, or learn from the process.

If you want to read a classic and care about how it looks on the page —
[browse our catalog](/ebooks/). If you want to help, the door is open.
